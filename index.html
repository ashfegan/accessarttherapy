Hello, I’m Aisling (Ash-ling), an Irish artist and HCPC-registered art psychotherapist with over ten years’ experience with children and adults.

I work online with people who have often felt unseen or let down, including neurodivergent and queer people, and those living with the impact of institutional abuse, systems that failed them, and trauma passed down through families. Many of the people I support are living with depression, low self-esteem, disordered eating or climate anxiety. Together we make space to explore what you’re carrying, at your own pace, with art as a way in when words feel hard to find.

I have a particular specialism in working with Irish people living abroad, whether first, second or third generation, and the ways historic institutional harm can echo through families and identity.

My approach is psychodynamic and group analytic, shaped by the ethos of therapeutic communities. My background in community incident response, disaster recovery and the television and film industry means I’m comfortable alongside people who have lived through complex, high-pressure or unsettling experiences.

If you’d like to explore whether working together might help, get in touch for an initial conversation.

Art Psychotherapy

Art psychotherapy (also known as art therapy) combines psychotherapy with art-making, such as drawing, painting or sculpting, to help you explore difficult thoughts, feelings and experiences. You don’t need to be artistic. What matters is the process, and what emerges as you create. Because it doesn’t rely on words alone, it can be especially helpful when trauma is hard to put into language.

Art psychotherapists are registered with the HCPC (Health and Care Professions Council) — the statutory regulator for the profession in the UK. This register ensures safety and best practice by holding registrants to set standards of proficiency, training, and conduct.

Supervision

Supervision is a supportive space to think together about your work: your clients, the groups and settings you work in, and the wider world they sit within. Its purpose is to protect client welfare and to support your development as an art psychotherapist.

My approach is conversational, psychodynamic and group analytic. We learn through sharing experience and reflecting together. Creative responses are welcome, and we can make art in supervision if you’d like. You’re also welcome to bring how the work and your training are affecting you, professionally and personally.

I see supervision as a nourishing space, not a tick-box exercise, with ethical practice at its heart. It helps psychotherapists sustain their work over time.

For community organisations, supervision gives your team reflective space and helps keep your work trauma-informed. It adds a safeguarding layer, supports learning and helps protect against burnout.
